How the calculator works.

Step 1: your resting energy. It uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation. For women, that's 10 × weight in kg + 6.25 × height in cm − 5 × age − 161. For men, the last number is +5.

Step 2: your maintenance. It multiplies that by your activity level: 1.2, 1.375, 1.55 or 1.725.

Step 3: your deficit. It takes off 250, 500 or 750 Calories a day. It uses the rule of thumb that 1 kg of weight change is about 7,000 Calories, so 500 a day is about 0.5 kg a week. Other sources use up to 7,700, and real bodies don't follow any rule exactly.

Quick answers.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

Start from your maintenance, the number this calculator estimates, and eat a little less than that. A common starting point is about 500 Calories a day under maintenance.

What's a safe calorie deficit?

That depends on you, so talk to your GP if you're unsure. Harvard Health says calorie intake shouldn't fall below 1,200 a day for women or 1,500 for men, except under a health professional's supervision. This calculator won't show a target below those.

How do I convert calories to kilojoules?

Multiply Calories by 4.184. So 1,500 Calories is about 6,280 kJ. Australian food labels usually show kilojoules.

Who shouldn't use this?

It isn't for anyone under 18, pregnant or breastfeeding, or living with or recovering from an eating disorder. If you have a medical condition or take medication that affects your weight, ask your doctor first.

Is this the same number the CalCut app gives me?

Not necessarily. CalCut sets its own target when you start. This page is a separate estimate, so the two can differ.
